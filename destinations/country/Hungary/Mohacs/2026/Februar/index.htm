Hungary - Mohacs

Busojaras Festival in Mohacs

Visited in Februar 2026 - Best season: Late winter

Mohacs is one of Hungary's most recognizable cultural stops thanks to Busojaras, a traditional festival with carved masks, loud street rituals, and a strong local folklore identity.

What Did I Find?

Arriving in Mohacs is an exciting experience. As soon as you enter the town, one main street leads you toward the Danube. The moment you step into the cordoned-off festival zone, masked young people greet you with loud clappers: metal bells and wooden rattles that crack when spun by hand. At first, everything feels a little chaotic, but for a reason.
This pagan custom is used to chase winter away and announce the arrival of spring. Everyone looks happy, energized, and genuinely involved. Some locals even welcome visitors with a bottle of wine and offer a sip - that happened to me.
The girls wear a kind of folk costume with wide skirts and thick knitted socks, wrapped in black wool capes. Over their faces they wear lace scarves, with hair tied in buns and bright makeup.
1/2
One custom is to sprinkle flour on visitors. I tried to ask what it means, but I did not get an answer.
Food is a big part of Busojaras, so if you want to eat like a local, these are my top three picks. First, try sokac bab, the famous Mohacs-style bean stew cooked in clay pots over open fire, rich with smoked meats and paprika flavors. Second, look for csiptetett ponty, skewered carp roasted by the fire - a traditional fish option linked to local Danube culture. Third, finish with carnival pastries: farsangi fank or csorogefank, usually served with apricot jam and powdered sugar.
If you taste only three things during the festival, these give you the best mix of local tradition, street-fair atmosphere, and the sweet carnival side of Mohacs.
