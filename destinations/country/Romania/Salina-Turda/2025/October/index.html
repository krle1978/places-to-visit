Salina Turda: Romania’s Underground Cathedral of Salt

Visited in October 2025 • Best season: Autumn

Salina Turda is not just a salt mine — it's one of the most surreal underground places in Europe. Vast chambers carved entirely from salt, a bright underground lake, curved wooden structures and echoing silence make this place feel like another world.

What Did I Find?



We arrived at Salina Turda around 2 pm and had two hours to explore and enjoy the place. The visit begins with an elevator ride that takes you 120 meters underground into the salt mine.

Inside, it feels like a youth entertainment center carved into the earth complete with a lake and wooden boats. Naturally, we decided to take a 30-minute rowing session: I rowed, while my companion mostly cheered, took photos, and recorded videos.

1/2

You don’t have to row, of course. You can simply walk across bridges that connect various wooden platforms shaped like small pavilions. It’s also great for taking selfies with the dramatic salt-covered walls. There are even sports areas, including table tennis and mini-football.

The air inside the mine is incredibly healthy, Salina Turda is used as a natural “air spa.” Historically, miners working there since 1075 reportedly had no lung diseases at all due to the therapeutic salt-rich atmosphere.
